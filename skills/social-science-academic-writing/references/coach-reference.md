# Coach Reference

Use this file for detailed methods, templates, and examples. Keep the active answer concise; draw only the parts needed for the user's task.

## Frameworks

Belcher:
- Best for journal article revision and submission.
- Move from argument to abstract, journal fit, literature, significance, evidence, structure, opening/conclusion, sentences, submission.
- Core question: is this draft becoming a publishable article rather than a good course paper or chapter?

Craft of Research:
- Best for argument logic.
- Map claim, reasons, evidence, warrants, objections, and responses.
- Core question: what problem does this solve for readers?

Becker:
- Best for stuck writers and clunky social science prose.
- Draft badly first, then rewrite. Name actors and actions. Cut status language.
- Core question: what are you really trying to say?

Weisbach:
- Best for paper presentation.
- Readers skim titles, abstracts, introductions, headings, tables, and conclusions.
- Core question: can a busy reader quickly see the question, contribution, evidence, and result?

## Section Functions

| Section | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Signal topic and angle. |
| Abstract | State problem, evidence, claim, and contribution. |
| Introduction | Motivate problem, state claim, explain contribution, preview structure. |
| Literature review | Create an opening in a debate, not a source list. |
| Theory/framework | Define the concepts that organize interpretation. |
| Methods/data | Explain how evidence was produced and bounded. |
| Findings/analysis | Present and interpret evidence in support of claims. |
| Discussion | Explain implications, alternatives, and limits. |
| Conclusion | State what readers now understand differently. |

## Quick Rubric

Rate each as `strong`, `workable`, or `needs revision`:

- Main claim is specific, contestable, and visible.
- Contribution is more than "no one has studied this."
- Literature review creates an opening.
- Evidence is interpreted, not just reported.
- Objections and limits are handled fairly.
- Sections and paragraphs have clear jobs.
- Prose names actors, actions, mechanisms, and stakes.
- Hedges match the evidence.

## Templates

Argument map:

```markdown
Main claim:
Reason:
Evidence:
Warrant:
Likely objection:
Response:
Contribution:
```

Contribution builder:

```markdown
Topic:
Evidence/case:
Current conversation:
What is missing or mistaken:
Main claim:
What changes if readers accept it:
One-sentence contribution:
```

Abstract:

```text
This article examines [problem]. Using [evidence], it argues that [claim]. The analysis shows [mechanism or pattern]. This matters because [field-level contribution].
```

Literature paragraph:

```text
Research on [A] shows [consensus]. Work on [B] complicates this by showing [tension]. Yet these approaches leave unresolved [gap]. This article addresses that opening by [contribution].
```

Results interpretation:

```text
[Table/Figure/Excerpt] shows [pattern]. This matters because [interpretation]. It supports the larger claim by [link]. A limit is [limit], so the claim is best understood as [qualified claim].
```

Reviewer response:

```text
Thank you for this suggestion. I have revised [section] to [change]. The revised text now [effect]. This appears in [location].
```

## Before/After Examples

Topic to argument:

Before:
```text
This paper examines social media use among migrant workers.
```

After:
```text
This article argues that migrant workers use neighborhood messaging groups not only to exchange practical information, but also to create informal systems of workplace accountability that local labor organizations later draw on.
```

Weak significance to strong significance:

Before:
```text
This case has not been studied before.
```

After:
```text
This case shows why theories of neighborhood change need to account for informal brokerage, not only formal investment.
```

Literature list to opening:

Before:
```text
Smith studies eviction in Milwaukee. Garcia examines landlord strategies. Chen analyzes housing courts. Patel studies tenant organizing.
```

After:
```text
Research on eviction has shown that forced displacement deepens poverty and destabilizes families. A second line of work shifts attention to the institutional settings that make eviction routine, including landlord strategies and housing court procedures. Less attention has been paid to the period before formal filing, when tenants respond to threats, fees, and inspections that may never appear in court records.
```

Results reporting to interpretation:

Before:
```text
Table 2 shows the results. The coefficient is significant in Models 1 and 2 but not Model 3.
```

After:
```text
Table 2 shows that neighborhood organization is associated with lower reported eviction pressure in the baseline models. The association weakens after adding landlord fixed effects in Model 3, suggesting that organizational density may partly capture differences in landlord practices rather than only neighborhood-level support.
```

Inflated prose to clear prose:

Before:
```text
The implementation of disciplinary procedures resulted in the production of compliance among students through institutional mechanisms of surveillance.
```

After:
```text
Teachers produced compliance by recording minor rule violations and sending weekly behavior reports to parents.
```

Hedging:

Too weak:
```text
These interviews may perhaps suggest that workers could potentially develop some forms of solidarity.
```

Too strong:
```text
These interviews prove that platform workers are highly solidaristic.
```

Better:
```text
These interviews show that workers developed practical forms of solidarity around information sharing, while stopping short of formal collective organization.
```

## Diagnostic Questions

- What is the one-sentence argument?
- What would a skeptical reader dispute?
- Why should this journal or field care?
- Which evidence carries the most weight?
- What does the evidence mean, not just show?
- What assumption connects evidence to claim?
- What alternative explanation should be addressed?
- Which section delays the argument?
- Which paragraph has no clear job?
- Which words are performing academic status rather than analytic work?
- Who acts in this sentence?
- What can be cut without changing the meaning?
